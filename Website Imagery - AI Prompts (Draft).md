## Shared style guide (paste this context first, or fold it into each prompt)

Keep every image consistent with this so they feel like one cohesive site, even generated across separate sessions:

- **Mood:** premium but grounded — a trusted local trade business, not a corporate stock-photo brand. Warm, honest, competent. Avoid anything that reads as a generic "smiling exterminator" stock photo — it looks fake and undermines trust.
- **Colour cues:** brand green `#6AA84F`, deep green `#2F5723`/`#3C6B2C`, and on dark sections a brighter accent green `#8ACF63` against near-black `#0C160E`–`#17301F`. Warm natural light (golden hour, soft daylight) works well against these greens.
- **Setting:** Perth / Western Australia — think red brick or Colorbond homes, jarrah and gum trees, sandy light-coloured soil, blue skies, established suburban gardens. Avoid anything that reads as a different climate (no snow, no dense green English-style gardens).
- **People:** avoid visible faces or identifiable people. Hands-only, from-behind, or equipment/environment-only shots read as more authentic and keep things anonymous. No stock-photo-style posed "worker smiling at camera."
- **Avoid:** any real pesticide/chemical brand names or logos, any legible small text or signage (AI image tools render text badly — see note below), number plates, and anything that looks like a specific real address.
- **Format:** landscape, roughly **5:4** ratio (e.g. 1600×1280px) unless noted otherwise, to match the site's image frames.

**A heads-up on two slots I've deliberately left out below:** the "Established 1993" seal and the "REG 1229 licensed" badge on the About page both rely on small, precise, legible text. AI image generators are unreliable at rendering text cleanly — you'll likely get garbled or misspelled words. My recommendation is to **keep the current custom-made graphics** for those two rather than spend credits on an AI attempt. If you want new versions later, that's a quick graphic-design job, not an AI-image job.

## 1. Home page — "Why Farrmill" section (advanced technology visual)

**Where it lives:** Home page, dark green band titled "Why Farrmill," right-hand illustration.
**What it replaces:** an abstract radar/detection graphic sweeping over a house icon.
**What it needs to convey:** careful, technology-assisted inspection — without overstating one specific device (the copy deliberately says "advanced technology when the job calls for it," not any one named tool).

> A close-up, detail photograph of a pest control technician's hands using a handheld inspection tool (such as a moisture meter or borescope) against a timber wall frame or subfloor timber, in a Perth home under renovation or construction. No face visible — hands and forearms only, wearing a plain dark workwear sleeve. Warm indoor lighting with a subtle green glow from the device's screen. Shallow depth of field, shot on a 50mm lens look, photorealistic, professional and quietly technical mood. Landscape orientation, 5:4 ratio.

## 6. Home hero / ute backdrop

**Where it could live:** the Home page hero (currently a dark glass card with text), or a new "our vehicle" moment on the About or Contact page — your call once you see how it looks.

**How this one's different:** this prompt generates an *empty backdrop scene* for you to composite your own ute photo into afterwards (e.g. in Photoshop, Canva, or an AI compositing tool) — it should not include any vehicle itself.

> A wide photograph of a suburban Perth street or driveway at golden hour — warm low sunlight, jarrah or gum trees in the background, light sandy-coloured paving or asphalt in the foreground, blue-gold sky. The scene should be empty of any vehicles, people, or signage, with clear open space in the centre-foreground where a vehicle will be added afterwards. Photorealistic, warm and inviting, slightly cinematic golden-hour lighting. Wide landscape orientation, 16:9 ratio.

**Tip for the compositing step:** when you take the real photo of your ute, try to match the lighting direction and time of day you used for this backdrop (e.g. also shoot it at golden hour, from a similar low angle) — it'll make the composite far more convincing.
